The journey of the farthest thing humans have ever built

# What is a light-day?

Light is the fastest thing there is: in **one second it zooms around the Earth 7½ times**.

A **light-day** is how far light travels in **one whole day**: **25.9 billion kilometres** (16 billion miles).

That's how far away Voyager 1 is now! A radio message takes **one day** to reach it – and you wait **two days** for the answer.

<!-- year: 18 November 2026 -->
<!-- notes: Speed of light about 300,000 km/s; Earth's circumference about 40,000 km → 7.5 laps per second. 299,792 km/s × 86,400 s = 25,902,068,371 km ≈ 173 astronomical units (Earth–Sun = 1 AU). Wow fact: driving at 100 km/h (60 mph) you would need almost 30,000 years for that distance. A light-year is 365 times as far as a light-day. -->

# Lift-off: 1977

![Launch of the Titan-Centaur rocket with Voyager 1 on top](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c6/Titan_3E_with_Voyager_1.jpg/1280px-Titan_3E_with_Voyager_1.jpg)
<!-- source: Titan IIIE-Centaur rocket with Voyager 1 – NASA (Wikimedia Commons) | https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Titan_3E_with_Voyager_1.jpg | Public domain (NASA) -->
<!-- year: 5 September 1977 -->

A huge **Titan rocket** carried Voyager 1 into space from Florida.
Its twin sister **Voyager 2** had launched 16 days earlier.

Voyager 1 has now been travelling for **49 years** – longer than most of your parents have been alive!

<!-- notes: Launched from Cape Canaveral (Florida, USA). The two probes used a rare line-up of the planets that let them reach Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune one after another. Voyager 1 took a faster path, so it reached Jupiter first despite launching later – that's why it is called "1". -->

# What can the probe do?

![The Voyager 1 spacecraft during launch preparations in 1977](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/98/Voyager_1_reencapsulated.jpg/1280px-Voyager_1_reencapsulated.jpg)
<!-- source: Voyager 1 during launch preparations at Kennedy Space Center, 1977 – NASA (Wikimedia Commons) | https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Voyager_1_reencapsulated.jpg | Public domain (NASA) -->
<!-- year: 1977 -->

- About as heavy as a **small car** (around 800 kg)
- An **antenna as big as a trampoline** (3.7 m) radios home to Earth
- Power comes from a **"nuclear battery"** – sunlight is far too weak out there
- It flies **17 km every second**

<!-- notes: More numbers (NASA figures, rounded): the battery (radioisotope generator) makes electricity from the heat of plutonium; it delivers about 4 watts less every year and is now down to roughly half its power at launch. The radio transmitter has about 23 watts – like a fridge light bulb. The computer has only about 69 kilobytes of memory – a single phone photo is much bigger. Speed about 61,000 km/h (38,000 mph): fast enough to cross the whole United States in about five minutes. -->

# The first photo of Earth and Moon together

![Earth and Moon as two thin crescents in black space](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/de/PIA00013_Crescent_Earth_and_Moon.jpg)

<!-- year: 18 September 1977 -->

Soon after launch, Voyager 1 turned around and took this picture:
**Earth and Moon together in one photo** – nobody had ever seen that before.

By then it was already almost 12 million kilometres away.

<!-- notes: Taken from 11.66 million km. The Moon was made three times brighter in the picture so you can see it at all next to the bright Earth. Earth is at the bottom, the Moon at the top. -->

# Jupiter: a storm bigger than Earth

![Jupiter's Great Red Spot, a giant storm, photographed by Voyager 1](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f8/PIA00022_Jupiter_Great_Red_Spot_Mosaic.jpg/1280px-PIA00022_Jupiter_Great_Red_Spot_Mosaic.jpg)
<!-- source: Jupiter's Great Red Spot, Voyager 1 – NASA/JPL (Wikimedia Commons) | https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:PIA00022_Jupiter_Great_Red_Spot_Mosaic.jpg | Public domain (NASA) -->
<!-- year: March 1979 -->

The **Great Red Spot** is a giant storm – our whole Earth would fit inside it.

At Jupiter, Voyager 1 also discovered **lightning**, a very **thin ring** and two **new moons**.

<!-- notes: Closest approach to Jupiter on 5 March 1979. New moons: Metis and Thebe. More than 17,000 pictures were radioed back to Earth. Jupiter's gravity worked like a slingshot and sped Voyager up – heading for Saturn. -->

# Io: volcanoes on another moon!

![Jupiter's moon Io with a volcanic plume at its edge](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/70/Io_with_Loki_Plume_on_Bright_Limb_%28cropped%29.jpg)
<!-- source: Jupiter's moon Io with the volcanic plume of Loki, Voyager 1 – NASA/JPL/USGS (Wikimedia Commons) | https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Io_with_Loki_Plume_on_Bright_Limb_(cropped).jpg | Public domain (NASA) -->
<!-- year: 5 March 1979 -->

The biggest surprise: Jupiter's moon **Io** has **erupting volcanoes**!
For the first time, people saw **active volcanoes on a world other than Earth**.

Can you see the cloud at the edge? That's a volcano called **Loki**.

<!-- notes: Voyager 1 found 9 active volcanoes on Io. The first volcanic plume was spotted by a navigation engineer (Linda Morabito), who was actually only looking for stars in a picture to work out the probe's position. Io is constantly squeezed by Jupiter's gravity – that heats up its inside. -->

# Saturn and its mysterious moon Titan

![Saturn as a crescent, photographed by Voyager 1 after the flyby](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b2/Crescent_Saturn_as_seen_from_Voyager_1.jpg)
<!-- source: Crescent Saturn, Voyager 1 – NASA (Wikimedia Commons) | https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Crescent_Saturn_as_seen_from_Voyager_1.jpg | Public domain (NASA) -->
<!-- year: November 1980 -->

Saturn's rings are made of **thousands of narrow ringlets** of ice and rock.

The moon **Titan** is wrapped in **thick orange haze** – you can't see down to the ground at all.

After that, Voyager 1 flew **up and away** out of the plane of the planets – out into the wide open.

<!-- notes: Closest approach to Saturn on 12 November 1980. This picture was taken four days later, looking back towards the Sun – a view you can never get from Earth. To fly close past Titan, the team accepted that Voyager 1 could not visit any more planets. Voyager 2 went on to Uranus and Neptune. -->

# Carl Sagan – the star explainer

![Carl Sagan with photos of Mars](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7a/P-13734B_Carl_Sagan.jpg/1280px-P-13734B_Carl_Sagan.jpg)
<!-- source: Carl Sagan with Mars photos from the Mariner 9 probe – NASA/JPL (Wikimedia Commons) | https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:P-13734B_Carl_Sagan.jpg | Public domain (NASA) -->

Carl Sagan was an American astronomer. He explained space to millions of people on TV.

He wondered: **What if aliens find Voyager one day?**
His answer: Let's send along a **letter from Earth**!

<!-- notes: Carl Sagan (1934–1996) worked on many NASA missions. He led the small team that put together the Golden Record. In the photo he is holding pictures from the Mars probe Mariner 9. -->

# The Golden Record

![The gold-plated cover of the Voyager record with engraved drawings](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/56/The_Sounds_of_Earth_Record_Cover_-_GPN-2000-001978.jpg/1280px-The_Sounds_of_Earth_Record_Cover_-_GPN-2000-001978.jpg)
<!-- source: Cover of the Golden Record "The Sounds of Earth" – NASA/JPL (Wikimedia Commons) | https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Sounds_of_Earth_Record_Cover_-_GPN-2000-001978.jpg | Public domain (NASA) -->
<!-- year: 1977 -->

A gold-plated record, as big as a plate (30 cm). On it:

- **Greetings in 55 languages** – in English: "Hello from the children of planet Earth"
- **Sounds of Earth**: thunder, whale song, laughter, a heartbeat
- **Music** from all over the world and **115 pictures**

The drawings on the cover explain **how to play the record**.

<!-- notes: The record is made of copper and gold-plated; a needle to play it is included. About 90 minutes of music, from Bach and Beethoven to Chuck Berry and music from many countries. The English greeting was spoken by a child (Nick Sagan, Carl's son). The German greeting is "Herzliche Grüße an alle" (warm greetings to everyone). The drawings show, among other things, how fast the record must spin and where our Sun is. Question for the class: "What would you put on a record like this?" -->

<!-- year: 14 February 1990 -->

Carl Sagan had another idea: Turn the camera back towards Earth **one last time**!

From 6 billion kilometres, our Earth is just **a tiny blue dot** – smaller than a pixel.
Everyone who has ever lived was on this little speck.

<!-- notes: The dot is in the bright band, roughly middle right. The bands are sunlight scattered inside the camera. On the same day a "family portrait" with six planets was taken. Shortly afterwards the cameras were switched off for good to save power. Sagan wrote a book about it called "Pale Blue Dot". -->

# If Earth were a walnut …

- 🌍 **Earth** = a walnut (3 cm)
- 🌙 **Moon** = a pea, almost **1 metre** away
- ☀️ **Sun** = a ball **taller than your classroom** (3.3 m) – and **350 metres** away, more than 3 football pitches!

Light needs 1 second from the Moon to us, and 8 minutes from the Sun.

<!-- notes: Scale: 1 cm stands for 4,247 km (Earth's diameter 12,742 km ↦ 3 cm). To convert: real distance in km divided by 4,247 = centimetres in the model. Values: Moon 3,474 km ↦ 0.8 cm, distance 384,400 km ↦ 90 cm. Sun 1.39 million km ↦ 3.28 m, distance 149.6 million km ↦ 352 m. Light travel times: Moon 1.3 s, Sun 8 min 20 s. Tip: swap the distances for places near you (e.g. "from school to the bakery"). Hands-on: bring a real walnut and a pea. -->

# … then Voyager 1 would be 61 km away!

Measured from the Sun:

- 🔴 **Mars** = a hazelnut, **540 m** away
- 🟠 **Jupiter** = a big pumpkin, **1.8 km** away
- 🔵 **Neptune**, the last planet: **10½ km**
- 🛰️ **Voyager 1: 61 km** – and **smaller than a virus**!

The nearest star would be **95,000 km** away – more than **twice around the real Earth**.

<!-- notes: Values (1 cm = 4,247 km): Mars 6,779 km ↦ 1.6 cm, distance from Sun 228 million km ↦ 537 m. Jupiter 139,820 km ↦ 33 cm, distance 778 million km ↦ 1.83 km. Neptune 4.5 billion km ↦ 10.6 km. Voyager 1 (1 light-day = 25.9 billion km) ↦ 61 km (38 miles), about an hour on the motorway. Its 3.7 m antenna would be only 9 nanometres across. In the model Voyager crawls only 14 cm per hour – slower than a snail – and needed 49 years for the 61 km. Nearest star Proxima Centauri: 4.24 light-years ↦ about 94,700 km. -->

# Out of the "Sun bubble"

![Drawing: the Sun in the middle of a blue bubble, Voyager 1 and 2 flying at its edge](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/17/Voyager_final_region_before_interstellar_space.jpg/1280px-Voyager_final_region_before_interstellar_space.jpg)
<!-- source: Voyager 1 and 2 at the edge of the heliosphere (illustration, 2012) – NASA/JPL-Caltech (Wikimedia Commons) | https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Voyager_final_region_before_interstellar_space.jpg | Public domain (NASA) -->
<!-- year: 25 August 2012 -->

The Sun constantly blows a wind of tiny particles into space. It forms a giant **bubble** around all the planets.

In 2012, Voyager 1 flew through the wall of this bubble – the **first human-made object in the space between the stars**.

<!-- notes: In the picture: the yellow dot in the middle is the Sun, the bubble around it is the heliosphere (Termination Shock = where the solar wind slows down, Heliosheath = outer layer of the bubble, Interstellar Wind = the wind between the stars). Voyager 1 is the upper probe. The drawing is from December 2012. The bubble is called the heliosphere, its boundary the heliopause. Voyager 2 followed in November 2018. The crossing showed up in the instruments: the particles outside are different from those in the solar wind. Note: the Sun's gravity reaches much farther – so "edge of the solar system" depends on what you mean. -->

# What happens next?

The nuclear battery gets **weaker** every year, so the team switches off one instrument after another.
In a few years, Voyager 1 will **fall silent forever**.

But it keeps flying: in about **40,000 years** it will pass another star –
with the Golden Record on board. 🌟

**Any questions?**

<!-- notes: Status November 2026: only two instruments are still running (magnetometer and plasma wave instrument). The cosmic ray instrument was switched off in February 2025, the low-energy charged particle instrument in April 2026. In October 2026 there was no contact for two days because a set of thrusters had stopped working – backup thrusters took over. NASA expects radio contact to end sometime in the 2030s. The star is called Gliese 445 (in the constellation Ursa Minor, the Little Bear); Voyager will come within about 1.6 light-years of it – still very far away. -->
